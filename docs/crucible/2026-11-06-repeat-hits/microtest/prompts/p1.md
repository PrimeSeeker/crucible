This is a writing exercise on a past spec campaign's text; the finding below is reconstructed for it. Take the role of the campaign's controller. Below are the reference a controller works under for writing fix lists, a passage of the spec, what earlier rounds' fix lists did to that passage, and one finding from this round, taken as accepted at merge. Write this finding's fix-list item.

Give, in this order: the complete text of the passage as it will read after your fix (or `## Passage unchanged` if you would change it otherwise), then at most three lines saying what the item changes. Other sites in the spec are out of view: assume the sweep and trace turn up nothing further, and leave them out.

# The reference

## Fix application and blind verification

Write the **fix list**, the blind verifier's sole mandate input, into the round record's `## Fix list`, then apply it to the artifact. A fix list or plan-phase repair takes BLOCKER and MAJOR findings and **MINORs against binding text**, those whose fix changes the artifact's own binding content (a spec's: `skills/subagent-driven-development/references/review-terms.md` § What binds in a spec; a plan's: its Inter-task contracts and each task's Contract and Acceptance). A child's fix list never changes umbrella content. Deferral markers, adopted improvements, operator rulings and contained amendments ride it too; other MINORs follow `crucible:redteaming` § Merge and finding hygiene. **Fix-by-deletion** is a first-class resolution: all else equal, removing machinery outranks adding it.

**Fix altitude** (binding): every fix states its binding outcome unambiguously, at the altitude of the text around it, adding no mechanism that text lacks. Where a fix changes required behavior and its finding carries a reproducer (an input and the wrong result), the item adds an acceptance item: that input and the required result. A deferred finding's item lands the outcome, a `Deferred to implementation:` marker and any such acceptance item, not the mechanism. Outcome and acceptance item are binding content; the marker's owning task (its placement, or a Contract naming a spec marker) is only an ownership pointer.

The artifact states the current design only: a fix replaces text, never annotates it. No finding citations, resolution sketches, or amendment or changelog prose; what changed, when and why go in the round records. The marker line a scoped amendment writes stays.

**Propagation sweep**, before the list freezes: for each item, search the artifact by the changed content's key terms for every other site restating it, and list those sites in the item. Content genuinely stated once yields an item naming its one site and no sweep prose. A fix that lands at fewer sites than its item names is not landed.

**Trace** each rule an item adds or changes, a seat's suggested wording included, in every fix list and repair, before the list freezes: run `crucible:brainstorming` § Spec self-review's consumer sweep (who produces the rule's input, who reads its output) and trace (which routes and phases reach it), and list each artifact site they find among the item's restating sites.

A predicate is run, not read (binding): where an item's substance is a mechanical check (acceptance check, gate, grep, census query), run it at the tree state it will run against before recording the item applied, and put the command and its output under the item. A check of a state the change has not yet reached must fail on today's tree: that failure shows it can fire. Run a check adopted from a seat's remedy the same way; the seat's word is not evidence. The verifier checks that the text landed; you, that the predicate is right.

After every apply but round 0's, dispatch the blind verifier (instrument `blind-verifier`, `skills/redteaming/verifier-prompt.md`) with the fix list and the diff only, **never rationale**, plus the tracked files a delta claim names as that claim's evidence; a delta claim is a new "the code does X" claim the wave introduced. Check yourself each delta claim it reports not checked.

**Repair, not a loop** (both phases): each item not landed or partly landed, and each delta claim found FALSE, takes one scoped repair edit and a re-check of it alone by whichever gave the failing verdict, a verifier or you; one still failing after its re-check is a decision ask. A FALSE delta claim a decision rests on is a decision ask without the repair, since correcting the sentence leaves the decision without its ground. Each untraced hunk, one the verifier traces to no item, takes the same repair by its revert, unless an item needs it: then name it under that item in the verifier block, with why. The verifier's re-check is one fresh `blind-verifier` over the wave's repairs, filled as its card states, writing `verifier-recheck.md`. A repair opens no wave and books no dispatch beyond its re-check.

Record the verdicts, your checks and each repair in the round record's `## Verifier block`, then set state `verified`, in a terminal-pass record as `round-record.md` states.


# The passage (D22)

- **D22 — Work left open is filed where the project keeps deferred work,
  or named dropped.** At SDD's Finish, the work left open (minors, leads,
  questions, declined improvements, the deferrals D23 brings) becomes one
  grouped entry per feature in the project's home for deferred work,
  each item in it, and those the controller judges not worth keeping
  named dropped there; no item takes a ruling of its own, so "no ruling
  per minor" stands. In the repository: committed on the branch beside
  the umbrella mark, before the suite, so the suite runs once on the tree
  that lands; no review reads it on a first pass through Finish, so the
  close readout names the entry, and where the loop reopens, the branch
  reviewer takes that commit as in scope, as it takes the umbrella mark.
  Outside the repository (an issue tracker): the readout proposes the
  entry, filed only on the operator's word. No home known: the readout
  proposes one. Unruled asks are not filed; the readout asks them. A
  later answer revises the entry when Finish restarts. The workspace is
  deleted only once every item is filed or named dropped: an unanswered
  filing proposal is not a drop, and the controller says the workspace
  stays. This reverses the recorded position that filing open work is
  left to the operator or the repository's conventions: the filing is
  the controller's step, made where the convention says.

# What earlier rounds' fix lists did to this passage

- Round 0's fix list: stated what happens in each kind of home (in the repository, outside it, none known), and that the workspace is deleted once the operator's integration decision has been carried out.
- Round 1's fix list: made the items one grouped entry per feature; added that no item takes a ruling of its own; added that the branch reviewer takes the commit as in scope where the loop reopens; added that unruled asks are not filed; added that the workspace stays until every item is filed or named dropped, and that an unanswered proposal is not a drop.

# The finding

FINDING R2-1 [MAJOR] Filing still strands items in two of D22's cases. In a project with no known home, the readout proposes one; in a project with an outside tracker, the readout proposes an entry. If the operator does not answer that proposal and integrates the branch through a pull request, nothing is filed anywhere, the workspace stays with no session left to delete it, and the workspace is gitignored, so the open items are one `git clean` away from being lost. Also, parked BLOCKER or MAJOR findings with their rulings are not in the parenthesis, so they have no place in the entry. Location: D22.
