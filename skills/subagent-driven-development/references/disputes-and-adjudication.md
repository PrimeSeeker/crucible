### Disputes and adjudication

**Who rules.** You rule on each disputed finding with your evidence — an implementer's NOT CONFIRMED, a finding you judge wrong, a plan conflict: one that collides with plan text, or a reviewer's finding that a binding item's literal reading reaches a case its author did not consider (is the plan or the work wrong?). A fresh adjudicator seat rules instead on any of these that touches binding content (the Deviations paragraph of `crucible:subagent-driven-development § The Task Loop`), as a literal-reading finding always does, or the Intent, and on every dispute about a diff you wrote: any commit you made yourself, such as a task whose ledger holds `Task <N>: inline` (`crucible:subagent-driven-development § Inline mode`) or Finish's open items, and any lane diff. A plan conflict that is a design judgment goes to the operator as a decision ask. A finding that both fails a verdict and conflicts with plan text is ruled on first as a plan conflict; if the ruling upholds the plan, the verdict failure stands and enters the loop.

**The adjudicator dispatch.** Disputes for it go in batches — one wave's, or those you raise together at any point — each batch to one fresh adjudicator seat, its prompt composed by you. Its inputs: each disputed finding verbatim with its id; the evidence for each (the fix report's, or yours); the requirements inputs the finding was graded against — the plan pointer in the task loop, the spec at its pinned revision and the plan in the branch review, the lane note at its pinned revision on the very-small lane; where the spec's preamble (on the lane, the note's) carries an `Umbrella:` line, the umbrellas up the chain, by path; and per finding, the range of the review that raised it — in the task loop, with the amendment commits inside it (`crucible:subagent-driven-development § Plan changes mid-execution`). Copy into its prompt `skills/subagent-driven-development/references/seat-rules.md` § What binds, § Binding changes and § Building the diff, the last applied to each finding's range in turn, each range's files named apart, in its scratch subdirectory: its report's path less `.md`, in the workspace's `scratch/` directory (`.crucible/<feature-slug>/scratch/…` from the worktree root). Its mandate: for each finding, UPHELD or DISMISSED with a ruling, marking any ruling that touches binding content; it reads only in the worktree and writes only its report file and its scratch subdirectory. The status check applies. Its report file:

- task loop: `sdd/reports/task-<N>-adjudication-<b>.md`, where `<b>` numbers the task's dispute batches; `sdd/reports/task-<N>-x<X>-adjudication-<b>.md` in re-run `<X>` after a revert;
- branch review: `sdd/branch-review/adjudication-<b>.md`, `<b>` numbering the branch review's batches;
- a replacement seat adds `-r<R>` before `.md`.

**Outcomes.** Write each to `## Adjudication rulings` — the finding, UPHELD or DISMISSED, the ruling and its reason, your evidence or the adjudicator's report path — so a resumed controller rebuilds the open set from the ledger. An upheld finding re-enters the loop, or after the task's third wave the operator ask (`crucible:subagent-driven-development § The Task Loop`), and cannot be disputed again. A dismissed finding is closed by its ruling. A ruling the adjudicator marks as touching binding content, or makes on a literal-reading finding, passes `skills/redteaming/references/flip-paths.md` § The containment test before you act on it — on the very-small lane, one changing the note's binding parts is the operator's, as its reference states: a contained one you record as your ruling, as that section states; any other goes to the operator as a decision ask: record it as `pending operator`, and the finding stays open until the answer. A ruling adds no wave.
