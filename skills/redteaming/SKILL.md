# Redteaming — review controller

## Overview

You are the review **controller**. A **spec campaign** is an iterated loop: dispatch a panel of fresh-context review seats at one artifact, merge their findings, apply fixes, verify them blind, read out to the operator, and repeat until the stop rule says converged — then run the terminal pass and hand off. Every step writes records; every judgment is computed from the records, never from conversation memory.

**A plan is not campaigned**: it gets the **plan phase** (§ The plan phase — one verification round), which reuses the dispatch composition, the merge and finding-hygiene rules, and the readout; everything from § Round 0 to § Stop rule and terminal pass describes the spec campaign except where it says otherwise. The folded preset runs no plan phase (§ The folded lane).

**The operator steers through decision asks without being required to read the artifact.** That is why the readouts exist and why the terminal pass checks each ask's fidelity to the findings behind it.

**Scope:** the panel apparatus exists for specs and plans, nothing else. Open-ended project-wide issue-mining is out of scope and gets its own framing. Whole-branch review of implemented code is not a campaign either; it is `crucible:requesting-code-review`'s.

**Announce at start:** "I'm using the crucible:redteaming skill to run the spec campaign." — or "… to run the plan phase."

**Dispatch references:** when this skill starts, and again after compaction, read from the plugin root `skills/subagent-driven-development/references/dispatch-composition.md`, the rules every dispatch shares, and your adapter's `skills/using-crucible/references/<runtime>-dispatch.md`; both bind as if written here.

## Inputs and state

- **The artifact**: one spec or one plan, at a fixed path and revision. Every seat in a round reads that one revision.
- **The feature workspace**: `<repo>/.crucible/<feature-slug>/`, provisioned at brainstorm exit (if missing: the provisioning step in `formats.md` § Feature workspace root and layout). Records live under its `campaigns/spec/` or `campaigns/plan/`.
- **The formats contract**: `skills/redteaming/references/formats.md` defines the workspace layout, the round record, the state vocabulary, the special records and the feature folder; records are composed by plain path-join from the repo root. If this skill and that file conflict, stop and report BLOCKED — never improvise a format.

**Round records and state lines** (producer: you, at each phase boundary; consumers: your rehydration, the stop rule, the blind verifier, the terminal pass): each `round-NN/record.md` carries a `state:` line, each state written only after its phase's outputs are on disk (`formats.md` § Round record format, field 2). **Rehydration:** after compaction, resume from the workspace — the current round is the highest record number, and its state line says where to pick up; a fix list recorded while state is short of `fix-applied` is re-checked item by item against the artifact before any re-apply. A resume at `state: readout` delivers the recorded readout again, without its trace; re-delivering a readout is harmless. In the plan phase or on the folded lane, re-read that route's reference first (§ The plan phase — one verification round, § The folded lane).

**Artifact freeze** (binding): the artifact is never edited while any seat, verifier or pass is reading it — fixes land between dispatches.

**Shared tree** (binding): `skills/subagent-driven-development/references/dispatch-composition.md` § Shared-tree rules bind every seat you start. The status check runs after every dispatch window — panel seats (one window per concurrent batch), the round-0 pre-pass, the blind verifier, the terminal pass, and the plan phase's pre-pass and coherence seats — each dispatched with your fixes committed. Your fix apply lands between windows, never inside one.

## Dispatch composition

Every seat you start — panel seat, round-0 pre-pass, blind verifier, terminal pass, and the plan phase's mechanical pre-pass and coherence seat — carries the seven-item dispatch composition in `skills/subagent-driven-development/references/dispatch-composition.md`. Its input paths are the artifact at its revision, the workspace paths its inputs live at, the supporting inputs its card lists, the umbrellas up the chain where the binding artifact's preamble carries an `Umbrella:` line (every seat but the blind verifier; that file's item 2) and, for the blind verifier, the tracked files a delta claim names; nothing else. The round-0 pre-pass is the one controller-composed dispatch here. **The binding pointer** differs for these seats: where the artifact under review is a **plan**, it is the spec the plan's header names, and the **blind verifier gets none**: the fix list is its whole normative input, and anything more destroys its blindness.

**Dispatching an instrument.** Three seats are instruments, each with a slot card beside its template: instrument `redteam-seat` (`seat-prompt.md`, `seat-prompt.slots.md`), instrument `blind-verifier` (`verifier-prompt.md`, `verifier-prompt.slots.md`) and instrument `terminal-pass` (`terminal-pass-prompt.md`, `terminal-pass-prompt.slots.md`). Fill the card as `skills/subagent-driven-development/references/dispatch-composition.md` § Filling a card states.

## Round 0 — mechanical pre-pass

This is the **spec campaign's** round 0; the plan phase runs its own mechanical pre-pass inside its single round and has no `round-00`.

On **every** spec campaign, before any panel, dispatch one standalone agent (it consumes no round against the cap) to produce a claim table — every "the code does X" claim, or with no code baseline every claim about the artifact's inputs (the request, the decision record, the documents it cites), → TRUE/FALSE with evidence — plus a liveness check (every declared mechanism has a producer and a consumer within the artifact) and execution of regex/CLI claims (run them, don't read them). Its prompt carries § Suite bound, copied from `skills/subagent-driven-development/references/seat-rules.md`; the scratch line, that code it runs goes under the workspace's `scratch/campaigns/spec/round-00/`, never the tracked tree; and a staked severity on each finding, which decides what its fix list takes (§ Fix application and blind verification). Record it as `round-00/` per the formats reference and apply its fix list before round 1. **No dedicated verifier for round 0** — the round-1 panel's full-scope read is the check on those fixes.

## Panels

**Panel size tracks artifact maturity AND artifact size — never a fixed number.** Calibration (advisory): fresh large artifact round 1 = 4–5 seats; small artifact = 1 seat carrying several lenses; a round reading the result of a wave that added, removed or re-shaped a major mechanism (§ Stop rule and terminal pass) = 1–2 seats. A 1-seat round is **general by default**.

**Lens roster** (a lens is a search-ordering hint, never a scope limit):

- **correctness** (does what the artifact says hold, on every path and input it covers)
- **evidence-and-inference** (are its claims verified and its measurements valid, and do its conclusions follow from them)
- **general** (unscoped) — at least one seat with no box on **every multi-seat round**.
- **contract-fidelity** (does each interface match what its producers and consumers do) — when interface density warrants.
- **right-sizing** (cost-benefit scope-cutting) — seated in round 1 and periodically after, not every round: re-seated mid-campaign, or when you judge the artifact has grown far enough to need it; judged by the scope-cut decisions it produces. Its seat carries the **over-subtraction guard**, attesting the areas it examined and judged right-sized (`skills/redteaming/seat-prompt.md`), and you and fix waves consult those attestations before applying any cut — cutting attested content requires a logged ruling in the round record. A proposed cut that would remove binding content, or a declared mechanism's only producer or consumer, is a finding against the cut, not a cut.
- **abstraction-level** — for specs (mechanism detail creeping into binding sections is a finding).
- **outside-the-frame** (does the design solve the request's problem, for a child with its umbrella's Intent and entry, or would another framing, or no change, serve better) — one seat of every spec campaign's round 1 only, beside general in a one-seat round 1.

Rotate lenses across rounds and mix them per artifact; add a lens for whatever this artifact makes load-bearing, its one-line description in the seat's lens slot as for a roster lens.

**Full-scope mandate always**: every seat reads the whole artifact every round; the lens only orders the search. **Agents never see each other's reports.**

**Seat models:** every dispatch runs on the default role but one: **the blind verifier runs on the bounded role** and never lower — checking a fix list against a diff is a bounded task, and its mandate is unchanged by the tier. **Round 1's swap:** in a spec campaign's round 1 with two or more seats on this host, the seat you judge most architecture-heavy runs on the alternate role in place of the default role; name it in the campaign shape you propose at brainstorming's campaign gate. A seat on another host neither counts toward those two nor is ever the swapped one, so a default-role seat always sits beside the swap; a replacement for a lost swapped seat keeps the alternate role. A one-seat round 1 has no swap, and neither has the plan phase. The roles, the alternate role's other triggers, model naming and effort follow `skills/subagent-driven-development/references/dispatch-composition.md` § Models and effort.

Dispatch each seat as instrument `redteam-seat` with its filled slot card (§ Dispatch composition); its report `seat-<id>.md` feeds your merge. **Seat ids are never reused within a feature**: every seat, a replacement included, takes an id no earlier seat of the feature has used, so the stop check traces each seat's forecast across rounds by its id. The one exception is the plan phase's pre-pass, whose fixed id `p0` a successor plan phase under supersede reuses. Seats of one round are independent by construction, so **start every seat the host admits before waiting for any of them**; the rest of the fan-out mechanics are `crucible:dispatching-parallel-agents`'s. Record the roster (seat id, model, effort where set, lens) in the round record before or at dispatch.

**Launch:** read the campaign shape the operator approved at brainstorming's campaign gate — the gate entry in the workspace's `brainstorm-decisions.md`, naming the seats with their lenses and models and the expected rounds. Announce the shape in the campaign-start readout, naming any departure from that entry, and **proceed — no approval wait**.

**Quorum and degraded rounds** (producer: the round record's quorum block; consumers: cap arithmetic and the stop rule): record every lost or crashed seat with what happened. A round that lost any dispatched seat is either **topped back up** (the lost seat re-dispatched) or explicitly recorded as **degraded**.

**Topping up** follows the replacement line of the shared-tree rules. The replacement writes a `seat-<id>.md` under a fresh id (above), never the lost seat's file. In-flight seats resume by their recorded identity rather than being re-dispatched, so record each seat's identity in the roster before relying on continuation. A degraded round never counts toward convergence and does not consume the cap; **two consecutive degraded rounds = infrastructure escalation to the operator**. A 1-seat round losing its seat is degraded by definition — a crashed seat is never read as a clean pass.

## Merge and finding hygiene

When all seats return (state `returned`), merge into the round record's `## Merged finding list`, one entry per finding in the shape `formats.md` § Round record format, field 5 fixes — its `origins:` element naming every per-seat finding id it accounts for, and its disposition from that field's vocabulary, **with a ruling recorded for every partly-applied, parked or rejected finding** (a partly-applied one's naming the part not acted on). Silent discard is forbidden; § Round readout says which of them the readout carries.

**Premise findings.** A seat finding labelled `premise` merges into an entry carrying that label after its severity (`formats.md` § Round record format, field 5), disposition `parked`, its ruling a decision ask to the operator, routed, carried and relabelled as `skills/redteaming/references/flip-paths.md` § Premise findings states.

**Merge from the indexes.** Every seat report opens with a `## Index` — one line per finding: id, severity, a one-sentence claim, an evidence pointer — an advisory merge aid that nothing checks against the findings. Read the indexes first and merge from them; open a finding's body only to author its fix, to rule on a contested finding or a collision between seats, or where its index line is not enough to rule. **When you open a body to author its fix, check each sub-point of it against the fix item**: an unaddressed sub-point is added to the item or ruled in the entry. For a BLOCKER or MAJOR entry the check covers the body of every origin, duplicates included; outside that, a duplicate of a finding you have read, a MINOR entry's duplicates and a MINOR you defer are ruled from their index lines. Questions are read in full. A merged entry whose fix reverses an earlier round's applied fix names that round and entry id in its substance.

**Deferral** (`deferred-to-implementation`) is the disposition, **at any severity**, of a finding whose resolution needs mechanism that is **local** — inside one implementer's natural scope. Non-local mechanism, as `crucible:writing-plans` § Deferring detail lists it, is never deferred: it lands in the artifact's Interfaces & boundaries (a plan's Inter-task contracts). A finding a fix list takes whose resolution corrects or deletes existing artifact text is `applied` — corrections land at artifact stage, because a shipped artifact is never silently edited — and stays `applied` when only its local mechanism is deferred, that mechanism riding a marker in the same fix-list item. A deferred finding stays visible three ways: it **keeps its row** in the merged list; its `Deferred to implementation:` marker gets an owning task (`crucible:writing-plans` § Deferring detail), which from plan time on the pre-pass's ownership closure checks; and the round readout carries the round's **deferred count**. A deferral covering binding content or non-local mechanism, or a misplaced one, is a finding against it; a recorded deferral's missing detail is none (the same section).

**A MINOR no fix list takes** (§ Fix application and blind verification) that you accept takes, in this order: the artifact's Interfaces & boundaries, by a fix-list item, where it names missing non-local mechanism; `deferred-to-implementation` where its resolution needs local mechanism; `parked` where it is a design judgment for the operator; otherwise **`deferred-minor`**: left unfixed in the artifact, it reaches the branch review's triage through the close that seeds the SDD ledger, or an umbrella's landing (§ Exit). A later round's finding repeating a `deferred-minor` closes as its duplicate, in an entry naming that round and entry, and is seeded once. A MINOR you find wrong or outside the finding scope stays `rejected`.

**Every finding and question is dispositioned at merge.** Every raw seat finding is merged into an entry, or closed as a duplicate of one and carried in the absorbing entry's `origins:` element; a lost seat's findings that reached disk are merged like any other's. Every seat question id gets a disposition in the record's `## Question dispositions` (`formats.md` § Round record format, field 5). **A question that doubts the request, what the Intent asks for or a firm decision is always carried to readout**: the operator settles such a doubt, not you, the Intent's author. Round 0's questions, answered in its readout, follow the same rule. **Adopted** is for an improvement you take as a lightly held change, named among the readout's technical calls: it rides this round's fix wave as a fix-list item naming the question id, and never opens a wave by itself; where the round has no wave, the improvement is carried to readout instead.

**Finding hygiene** binds every seat through its template, and you enforce it at merge: a staked severity on each finding, a BLOCKER without evidence a question, and a run transcript or `UNVERIFIED` on each code-shaped claim. **`UNVERIFIED` findings never enter a fix list as-is** — verify or discard them, the outcome on an indented continuation line of the entry.

**Finding scope** (`skills/subagent-driven-development/references/seat-rules.md` § Finding scope) binds every seat through its template, and you enforce it at merge: a finding raised outside it is still merged and accounted for, and dispositioned `rejected` with that ruling.

**Record hygiene** (the seat rule, turned on the records and readouts *you* author): a code-shaped claim in a fix list, a merged entry, a ruling, a verification outcome, a readout or a decision ask shows its evidence — what you read or ran — or carries `UNVERIFIED`, and that tag is the whole vocabulary here. It tags a *claim*; it never admits an `UNVERIFIED` *finding* to a fix list. A decision ask resting on an untagged unverified mechanical fact is a defect in the ask.

### Operating stance — baseline error rate

A baseline defect rate in controller-authored text is a design assumption of this process; blind verification and the terminal pass are budgeted for it. A caught error is the loop functioning, not a verdict on the author: report errors, your own included, in the flat register of any other finding — the fact, the correction, the consequence — editorializing about no one.

Because the rate is expected and non-punitive, disclosure against interest stays cheap — shading a record, not erring in it, is the only reportable failure *of the reporting duty itself*; every other reporting obligation in this skill stands unchanged, and this stance may never be cited to skip, soften, or summarize any of them.

## Fix application and blind verification

Write the **fix list** into the round record's `## Fix list` (producer: you; consumer: the blind verifier — its *sole* mandate input), then apply it to the artifact. **A fix list takes**, of the findings, BLOCKER and MAJOR ones and **MINORs against binding text**: a MINOR whose fix changes the artifact's own binding content — in a spec what `skills/redteaming/references/flip-paths.md` opens by stating binds, in a plan its Inter-task contracts and a task's required fields and acceptance; a child's fix list never changes umbrella content. Deferral markers, adopted improvements, operator rulings and contained amendments ride it too; other MINORs follow § Merge and finding hygiene. That holds for every fix list — round 0's, a numbered round's, the plan phase's, the spec terminal pass's follow-up wave's — and for a plan-phase repair. **Fix-by-deletion is a first-class resolution** — a fix that removes machinery outranks one that adds it, all else equal.

**Fix altitude** (binding on every item): a fix states its binding outcome at the altitude of the text around it, in as many sentences as that takes to be unambiguous, and carries no mechanism the surrounding section does not already carry at that level. A deferred finding's item lands that outcome, a `Deferred to implementation:` marker and any acceptance item below; the mechanism is the implementer's to design. **Reproducers travel as acceptance:** where a finding carries a concrete reproducer — an input and the wrong result — and its fix, deferred or landed, changes required behavior, the item adds an acceptance item stating the input and the required result. The outcome and the acceptance item are binding content, which the blind verifier checks like any item and every downstream reviewer grades as binding; the marker's owning task (its placement, or for a spec marker each owning Contract naming it) is only the ownership pointer.

**The artifact states the current design only.** A fix replaces text; it never annotates it. The artifact carries no finding citations, no resolution sketches and no amendment or changelog prose — what changed, in which round, and why live in the round records. The amendment marker a scoped amendment writes — on a shipped spec through SDD, or mid-campaign (§ Flip paths — load-bearing content mid-campaign or mid-execution) — is a marker line, not narrative, and stays.

**Propagation sweep — before the list freezes.** A fix edits content, and content is commonly stated in more than one place. For each item, sweep the artifact by the content's key terms for every other site restating what that item changes, and enumerate those sites in the item. Content genuinely stated once yields an item naming its one site and no sweep prose beyond it. A fix that lands at fewer sites than its item names is not landed.

**Trace each rule an item adds or changes — before the list freezes**, a seat's suggested wording included: run `crucible:brainstorming` § Spec self-review's consumer sweep (who produces the rule's input, who reads its output) and trace (which routes and phases reach it) on it, and name each artifact site they find among the item's restating sites. Every fix list and repair named above takes it.

**A predicate is run, not read** (binding). Where an item's substance is a predicate — an acceptance check, a gate, a grep, a census query, any mechanical check — run it at the tree state it will run against before you record the item applied, and put the command and its output under that item in the `## Fix list`. A check of a state the change has not yet reached must fail on today's tree: that failure is the evidence it can fire. A check adopted from a seat's remedy is run the same way — the seat's word is not evidence. Whether the text landed is the verifier's check; whether the predicate is right is yours.

After **every** apply, dispatch the **blind verifier**, instrument `blind-verifier`: inputs are the fix list + the diff **only, never rationale**, with the tracked files a delta claim names as that claim's evidence. It verdicts every item landed or not, checks that nothing outside the list moved, and gives each new "the code does X" claim the wave introduced, a **delta claim**, TRUE or FALSE with evidence, or not checked with the reason (`skills/redteaming/verifier-prompt.md`).

Check each delta claim the verifier reports not checked yourself; record its verdicts, and your outcome for each such claim, in the round record's `## Verifier block` (state `verified`).

## Round readout

Record it in the round record's `## Round readout` at every round close and deliver it to the operator as recorded, less its trace (producer: you; consumers: the operator's decisions, the terminal pass):

- key findings declared — every rejected, parked or partly-applied BLOCKER/MAJOR among them named in words the operator can act on, with its ruling;
- **technical calls made autonomously**; **design judgments as decision asks** (`crucible:using-crucible` § Decision asks);
- the round's **`deferred-to-implementation` count**;
- **the questions carried to readout** (§ Merge and finding hygiene), each quoted verbatim — omitted where none is;
- in every numbered spec-campaign round, the folded lane's and an umbrella's included, **whether overturning a major mechanism after code is still reasonably expected, and why** (§ Stop rule and terminal pass), answering each seat forecast that it is reasonably expected by the mechanism it names — omitted elsewhere: round 0, the terminal-pass record and the plan record are never the stop round;
- at the close of the plan phase (the plan record) and of a folded campaign (its terminal-pass record), **the tasks the plan marks `Review: branch only`**, each with its reason (`crucible:writing-plans` § Per-task structure — four fields, all required), listed for the operator as information, not an ask — omitted elsewhere, and where no task carries the mark.

That is the whole mandate — the items above, each omitted where its line says; omitted means dropped, not narrated as absent. Record hygiene (§ Merge and finding hygiene) binds how they are written; it is not another item. **Decision asks stand apart:** the readout delivers its asks last, in a block of their own after the other items, each with its options and recommendation; every other readout that puts decision asks to the operator and points here does the same. The delivered readout's own words carry no internal id — merged-list, seat-finding or question id; a carried question is quoted as written, ids included. What other readers need from them goes in the readout's **trace**, the record-only part after it, holding what `formats.md` § Round record format, field 8, lists.

**Readout coverage is yours:** beyond the terminal pass's check of carried questions, nothing checks it after you, so before recording the readout, check against the merged list that it covers every rejected, parked or partly-applied BLOCKER or MAJOR with its ruling, and against `## Question dispositions` that it quotes every question carried to readout. Then close the round: record the readout and its trace, write `state: readout`, and deliver the readout as recorded; the round is closed once it is delivered. An operator ruling or a record correction that arrives after a round closed is written, dated, into that round's `## Rulings and corrections`; an operator ruling also enters the records `formats.md` § Feature workspace root and layout names.

## Stop rule and terminal pass

**Stop trigger: the first numbered panel round after which overturning a major mechanism after code is no longer reasonably expected** — the overturn question, defined with its reading for an umbrella's campaign in `skills/subagent-driven-development/references/seat-rules.md` § The arrangement questions. The answer is **your judgment over the round's records** — its merged list, its fix list and the seats' forecast lines in their reports' `## Verdict` sections — never a mechanical threshold and never conversation memory. Severity is not the trigger: a BLOCKER that would overturn a mechanism keeps the campaign going by the question itself. Finding provenance and finding counts are not signals. **A round whose own fix wave adds, removes or re-shapes a major mechanism is never the stop round**: no seat has read the result. Recording a deferral never re-shapes one; the binding outcome a deferral's item lands is judged like any other edit. Once a campaign is chosen, at least one numbered panel round runs. **A premise finding is a decision ask, never an open finding anywhere in this section** (`skills/redteaming/references/flip-paths.md` § Premise findings).

The stop round's open findings a fix list takes (§ Fix application and blind verification) get that round's **one fix wave + blind verification**, backstopped by the terminal pass's full-scope re-read.

A campaign never converges on unreviewed edits: the **terminal pass (1 seat) always runs at campaign close**. Dispatch it as instrument `terminal-pass`, `phase` set `spec`; its inputs are the artifact, the campaign's round records and readouts, the brainstorm decision record (`brainstorm-decisions.md`), the seat findings behind each decision ask, each question carried to readout in its seat report and, for its stop check, every numbered round's seat reports, their `## Verdict` sections only. Its two mandates, the same in both phases, are an **artifact re-read**, full-scope where the final round produced a fix wave (its card's `fix-wave` selector), and **decision-ask fidelity**, the stop's basis and the decision record included in a spec campaign; they and their binding ordering are its template's (`skills/redteaming/terminal-pass-prompt.md`).

**Its report stays as the pass wrote it** — never transcribed, paraphrased, or trimmed by you. A correction, or a mismatch in the report's Notes, flagged `CONCLUSION-CHANGING` is itself a decision ask to the operator; an unflagged correction is reported and does not escalate. Its **bias line**, whether its corrections share a direction favoring the record's author, is a decision ask only where the pass states the record is **materially misleading**. Mechanics: the pass consumes no round against the cap (neither does round 0); its findings a fix list takes get **at most one further fix wave**. **One second read** (instrument `terminal-pass`, `follow-up` set `second-read`; the spec campaign's last report) runs after that wave and, record-only, whenever the pass's report carries a record correction or you put a decision ask to the operator again on it, checking the close readout you recorded in the terminal-pass record against that report and the seat findings behind each ask; one read covers both, and you deliver the close after it, its corrections added. Of what it leaves open, a MINOR not against binding text takes `deferred-minor`; the rest, and any BLOCKER or MAJOR still open at the cap, goes to the operator as a decision ask.

**Cap: 4 rounds (degraded rounds excluded, per § Panels), its guarantee that the campaign ends; the cap bounds rounds, not spend. Where overturning a major mechanism after code is still reasonably expected at the cap, or the cap round's own fix wave adds, removes or re-shapes one, that goes to the operator as a decision ask: another round / supersede / accept-with-parked-rulings.** Cap arithmetic is derived from records, never a stored counter: rounds consumed = count of records numbered ≥ 01 with `degraded: no`.

## The folded lane

**Where the brainstorm decision record carries the operator's confirmation of the folded preset** (`crucible:brainstorming` § Proposing the arrangement (step 4)), **read `skills/redteaming/references/folded-lane.md` in full before the campaign's round 0, and re-read it after compaction; it binds as if written here.** It carries the route's round-loop lines, its deferral owners, unfolding, and its exit.

## Exit

On convergence, the handoff is your **explicit move, stated in the close readout**. An open premise ask passes on at each close as `skills/redteaming/references/flip-paths.md` § Premise findings states, each bullet naming this close's step:

- a **spec campaign** hands off to `crucible:writing-plans`, an open premise ask carried in this close readout;
- **a spec campaign whose artifact is a combined spec+plan document names `crucible:subagent-driven-development` in its close readout, not the plan skill** — the combined document already is the plan (§ The folded lane). **That close also owns the ledger seeding** — open premise asks, `deferred-minor` entries and what earlier phases left open — in the forms `skills/redteaming/references/plan-phase.md` gives the plan phase's close;
- **a spec campaign whose artifact is an umbrella** (`<feature-folder>/umbrella.md`) **names the umbrella's landing in its close readout, not the plan skill**, and the umbrella lands by `skills/brainstorming/references/umbrellas.md` § Landing, open asks included;
- the **plan phase** hands off to `crucible:subagent-driven-development`, its close (ledger seeding, open premise asks and what earlier phases left open included) as written in § The plan phase — one verification round.

## Flip paths — load-bearing content mid-campaign or mid-execution

**Before raising a decision ask or taking a path on any of these, read `skills/redteaming/references/flip-paths.md` in full; it binds as if written here**: binding content a finding shows wrong from the spec campaign's close onward, which no fix wave flips; a supersede called for at any point, the cap ask's supersede option and an unfold included; a premise finding at any phase, the spec campaign included; a feature found to need splitting after its spec campaign closes; and, for a feature whose preamble carries an umbrella line, a binding change its umbrella needs or one landed on the base that the feature has not taken in, checked for at each round boundary. A feature found to need splitting before its spec campaign closes is no supersede: the campaign stops, and the feature goes back to brainstorming as `skills/brainstorming/references/umbrellas.md` § Children states. Re-read the reference after compaction: a flip in flight shows through its decision ask or your ruling, recorded in the round record, the plan record's fix list or the ledger you rehydrate from.
